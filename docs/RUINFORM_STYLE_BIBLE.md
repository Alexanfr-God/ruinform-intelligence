# RUINFORM STYLE BIBLE — v0.1

## Brand idea

RUINFORM is a post-consumer / post-apocalyptic design economy built around real surviving matter.

The world is not about ruin for spectacle. It is about value after waste: discarded objects become desirable artifacts, tools, furniture, lighting, and collectible design.

## Visual target

The final image should feel like:

- a real object made from real reclaimed matter
- contemporary collectible design
- photographed by an excellent editorial/product photographer
- physically believable enough to imagine building
- strange enough to feel authored
- visibly connected to the user's original objects

## What RUINFORM is NOT

- generic cyberpunk
- neon as the entire idea
- 2023 AI collage aesthetics
- random tactical straps
- costume apocalypse
- steampunk unless the source matter genuinely suggests it
- perfect glossy sci-fi surfaces
- generic Etsy DIY
- simple upcycling decoration

## Material language

Keep evidence of prior life:

- scratches
- faded fabric
- seams
- repaired edges
- exposed fasteners
- dents
- mismatched surfaces
- worn coatings
- glass imperfections
- old labels only when they are genuinely present and compositionally useful

Old matter should not become artificially pristine unless the concept deliberately creates tension between restored and worn surfaces.

## Formal language

Prefer:

- strong silhouette
- one dominant gesture
- asymmetry with purpose
- negative space
- compressed / exposed relationships
- precise interventions into worn matter
- soft vs rigid
- transparent vs opaque
- damaged vs exact
- domestic vs industrial
- protected vs exposed

## Photography language

Object first, world second.

Preferred environments:

1. SALVAGE STUDIO — dark practical workshop, quiet background, contemporary editorial light.
2. ARTIFACT DOCUMENTATION — neutral dark/grey field, museum-quality material lighting.
3. BUNKER DOMESTIC — worn but believable interior, restrained environmental context.
4. RECLAMATION GALLERY — minimal concrete/metal setting for premium collectible pieces.
5. WORKBENCH PROTOTYPE — honest tabletop / workshop presentation for buildable concepts.

Avoid fog, sparks, rubble, weapons, dramatic apocalypse props, and colored smoke unless the actual project requires them.

## Color

Source colors remain primary.

Accent light should reveal form rather than substitute for form.

Use color contrast deliberately. Do not automatically turn every electronic or lighting object into rainbow RGB.

## Success test

The finished piece should look like someone with excellent taste, limited resources, and access to a serious workshop deliberately transformed surviving matter into a new object.

The viewer should see both:

1. WHAT IT USED TO BE
2. WHAT IT HAS BECOME

Both are necessary for RUINFORM.
